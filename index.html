Beatbox
Sound Atlas

Every sound the archive holds, measured and classified the way a phonetician would classify it: where in the mouth it is made, which airstream drives it, what it does acoustically, and which instrument it stands in for. Two hundred recordings were analysed directly — the pitch, brightness, attack and decay figures on this site are measured from the audio, not estimated. The point of the exercise is the edge: where the human mouth reaches an instrument's territory, and where it simply cannot go.

Section 02 · Coverage

Articulatory coverage

Place of articulation across the top, manner down the side — the layout of an IPA consonant chart. Every cell is one way of making a sound. A shaded cell means spoken languages use that combination as a phoneme somewhere in the world. A coloured cell means this archive contains it, and the number is how many sounds. Cells outlined in ink are made by beatboxers but by no known language. Cells in pink are ruled out by anatomy: no human can make them at all. Hover a ruled-out cell to see why, and click any coloured cell to filter the atlas to it.

Sounds per place of articulation

The distribution is lopsided on purpose. Beatboxing leans on the lips and the tongue tip because those are the two articulators fast enough to keep sixteenth notes, and free enough to work while the throat holds a bass note underneath.

Section 03 · Measured sound

Pitch and spectrum

Both charts on this page are measurements, not estimates. Every recording was decoded and analysed: the fundamental by autocorrelation over the voiced frames of each file, the energy band by percentiles of the mean power spectrum. Section 06 · How to read this

Notation key

Standard Beatbox Notation is the community shorthand used in the pattern library and on every specimen card. It is not a standard in the institutional sense — it grew out of forum posts — but it is what beatboxers actually write.

IPA transcriptions on the specimen cards use the standard diacritics: ʼ for an ejective, ↓ for ingressive airflow, ˤ for pharyngealisation, ̥ for voicelessness, and the click letters ʘ ǀ ǃ ǂ ǁ. They are phonetic approximations of a musical gesture, not claims that a beatbox sound is a phoneme.
